Atkilt

Atkilt is a comforting, mildly spiced Ethiopian cabbage, potato, and carrot stew. Naturally vegan and gluten-free, its signature vibrant yellow color and warm flavor come from it is a comforting.

ingredients

  • 1 small head of green cabbage, roughly chopped or sliced
  • 3–4 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into bite-sized cubes
  • 3 medium carrots, sliced into thick rounds or sticks
  • 1 large onion, thinly sliced
  • ⅓ cup olive oil or vegetable oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, 1 teaspoon fresh ginger, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric and ground cumin
  • 1–2 green chilies or jalapeños, sliced (optional, for subtle heat)
  • ¼ to ½ cup water
  • 1 teaspoon salt (adjust to taste)
  • ½ teaspoon ground black pepper

steps

  1. Stir the Aromatics: Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onions and cook for 4–5 minutes until translucent. Stir in the minced garlic, ginger and optional green chilies, cooking for another minute until fragrant.
  2. Bloom the Spices: Add the turmeric, cumin, salt, and black pepper to the pot. Stir constantly for about 1 minute to toast the spices and infuse the oil.
  3. Cook the Root Vegetables: Toss in the carrots and potatoes, stirring well to coat them completely in the spiced oil. Pour in the water, cover the pot with a tight-fitting lid, and let it simmer for about 10–12 minutes until the potatoes begin to soften but are not fully cooked.
  4. Add the Cabbage: Stir the chopped cabbage into the pot. Cover again and turn the heat down to medium-low. Let the mixture steam in its own moisture for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. Finishing Touch: The dish is ready when the potatoes are fork-tender and the cabbage is soft but still retains a bit of its texture (not mushy). Taste and add more salt if needed.

Note: Serve your Atkilt Wat hot, ideally layered over spongy injera flatbread alongside other Ethiopian dishes like Misir Wat (lentil stew).
